- **CMT-DML:** CMT1 slows the distal segment as much as the rest of the nerve. EAN/PNS 2021 excludes the median wrist from its latency criterion; 150% is used here only as a magnitude benchmark.
- **CMT-DML (gap):** The residual latency (terminal branches, junction, muscle) is fixed per axon and never slows with demyelination, so distal latency barely rises. Fix: give the terminal segment its own demyelination factor.
- **CMT-TLI (gap):** Same cause as CMT-DML: the distal latency does not scale with the velocity, so the index climbs.
- **CMT-TYPICAL-NOBLOCK:** Uniform demyelination does not produce block or abnormal dispersion, even when severe.
- **CMT-TYPICAL-NOBLOCK (gap):** The model slows large axons the most, so at heavy demyelination the largest motor units arrive last and are cancelled by the earlier ones. The resulting drop meets the block criterion. Fix: scale velocities more evenly across fibre sizes in uniform disease.
